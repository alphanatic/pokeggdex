No.102タマタマ Pokédex Easter Egg Guide

10 Oct 2026

How this works

The site hides 10 surprises, called easter eggs. Nothing on screen points to them: you find each one by clicking something or typing something specific.

Every egg is a nod to the original Pokémon games from the late 1990s, so you don't need to know Pokémon to use this guide. Each egg below gives the exact steps, what you'll see, and a short "why it's there" explaining the reference.

Most eggs happen in the cabin, the pixel-art room around the Pokédex. Two places matter:

  • The porthole: the round brass window at the top right. Pokémon swim past it every 10–20 seconds.
  • The wall screen: the round Poké Ball screen on the left, which shows the Pokémon the Pokédex is on. It starts switched off: press the small red button at its lower-left and it links to the Pokédex after a moment.

When something has a message, a white text box appears in the bottom-left corner and types it out letter by letter. Click the box to skip to the next page, or wait and it moves on by itself.

Eggs you can find right now

1. Mew under the truck

  1. Look in the porthole. On the right is a wooden dock with a small white truck on it.
  2. Click the truck. A message says people think a Pokémon called Mew is hiding under it.
  3. Click it again. "RED used STRENGTH!" but the truck won't move.
  4. Click it a third time. Something pink darts out, and the Pokédex jumps to No.151, Mew.

Why it's there: in the original games, a truck sits next to the S.S. Anne cruise ship (the ship this cabin is on). Kids in 1999 spread a rumour that Mew, the rarest Pokémon, was hidden under it. It was never true.

2. MissingNo., the glitch Pokémon

  1. On the Pokédex's right half, press 0 three times on the blue keypad. Or press the 0 key three times on your keyboard, or type missingno in the search box and press Enter.
  2. The wall screen breaks into flickering stripes with a blocky "backwards L" shape.
  3. The Pokédex shows MISSINGNO., No.000, and a message says an item in your bag multiplied.
  4. Press ◀ or ▶ on the Pokédex to go back to normal.

Why it's there: MissingNo. ("missing number") is the most famous bug in Pokémon history. A trick in the original games made this broken creature appear, and meeting it gave you 128 extra copies of an item. Everything the Pokédex shows for it (its weight, its odd moves) is what the real games showed.

3. The Magikarp salesman

  1. Watch the porthole until an orange fish (Magikarp) leaps across the water. It's the most common visitor.
  2. Click it while it's in the air.
  3. A man offers to sell it to you for ¥500, then the fish uses its only move, Splash, and "nothing happened".

Why it's there: in the original games a man sells you a Magikarp for ¥500. It seems like a scam because the fish is useless, but it grows into Gyarados, one of the strongest Pokémon.

5. The old intro

  1. Leave the page alone for 45 seconds: don't move the mouse or press keys.
  2. The wall screen turns white and shows two Pokémon facing off: a purple Nidorino hopping on the left, and a grinning Gengar on the right.
  3. Move the mouse and it goes back to normal.

Why it's there: this battle is the opening animation of Pokémon Red and Blue, the first games.

6. A hint in the browser console

  1. Press F12 (or right-click the page and choose Inspect).
  2. Open the Console tab.
  3. A message says "Welcome aboard the S.S. ANNE!" and hints that you should try No.000, which leads you to egg 2.

Eggs that need luck or the right time

7. Shiny Pokémon (1 in 8192)

Every Pokémon that swims past the porthole has a 1 in 8192 chance of being "shiny": a rare alternate colour, with sparkles and a message. A shiny Gyarados (the big sea serpent) turns red instead of blue and asks why it isn't at the Lake of Rage.

At one visitor every 15 seconds or so, you'd wait about a day and a half on average, so most people never see one. That's the point.

Why it's there: 1 in 8192 is the real chance of a shiny in the games Gold and Silver. Those games also had one guaranteed red Gyarados, at a place called the Lake of Rage.

8. Surfing Pikachu

Now and then (about 1 visit in 30), Pikachu rides past the porthole on a surfboard. Click it and a message says it's off to Pikachu's Beach.

Why it's there: Pokémon Yellow had a surfing minigame called Pikachu's Beach.

9. Lapras on Fridays

On Fridays only (by your computer's clock), Lapras, a blue sea creature with a shell, can swim past the porthole. Click it for its message. On any other day it never appears.

Why it's there: in Gold, Silver and Crystal, a wild Lapras shows up in a cave called Union Cave, but only on Fridays.

10. Morning, day and night

The cabin follows your computer's clock:

Your timeThe cabin
4:00–9:59Pink sunrise in the porthole, lamp on
10:00–17:59Blue sky and clouds, lamp off
18:00–3:59Moon and stars, lit windows in town, dark room, glowing lamp

Why it's there: Gold and Silver were the first Pokémon games with a real clock, and they split the day into exactly these three parts.
